# Pattern-Break Failures — Resolved Forecasts Where the Historical Pattern Was Real but No Longer Transferable

**Snapshot: 21 August 2026.**

A useful historical-transfer benchmark needs examples where the source pattern is **not fabricated**. The strongest failures occur when:

```text
historical pattern is genuine
model detects it correctly
critical target-side difference is available in evidence
BUT model still transfers the old outcome with excessive confidence
```

FutureSearch's May 2026 analysis of BTF-2 failures provides three unusually clean examples of this exact failure mode.

Primary source: Dan Schwarz, **“History doesn't repeat itself as often as LLMs think,”** FutureSearch, 5 May 2026.  
Canonical: https://futuresearch.ai/blog/history-doesnt-repeat/

Evidence status: this is a research-team failure analysis/blog rather than a peer-reviewed standalone paper. The underlying questions are from the public BTF-2 benchmark and have resolved real-world outcomes.

---

# Case 1 — EU Iran nuclear sanctions: a real cadence whose generating mechanism had finished

## Target question

Would the EU Council adopt at least one further qualifying Iran nuclear-sanctions amendment between roughly mid-October and year-end 2025?

## Historical/local pattern

During 2025 the Council had repeatedly amended the sanctions instruments at roughly two-to-three-month intervals:

```text
February
April
July
September
```

This is not a hallucinated precedent. There really was a strong recent recurrence pattern.

## Model forecast

Claude Opus 4.6 forecasting agent:

```text
95% Yes
```

on two runs reported by FutureSearch.

## Outcome

No qualifying Council act occurred in Q4 2025. The next relevant action was in March 2026.

## Critical disanalogy / boundary condition

The repeated 2025 amendments were not generated by a stationary `Council acts every 2–3 months` process.

The September package was:

- comprehensive;
- tied to a hard external mid-October deadline;
- the culmination of the cadence.

Once that package was completed, the main legal/temporal driver of repeated amendments had been discharged.

The relevant historical abstraction was therefore not:

```text
repeated prior action → another repetition likely
```

but:

```text
deadline-driven sequence of actions
→ terminal comprehensive package
→ original recurrence mechanism ends
```

## Why this case is especially valuable

The model's own research had already surfaced the blocking condition. Its rationale reportedly noted that the September package was comprehensive and could reduce the immediate need for more action.

It nevertheless overrode that information with the historical cadence and treated a pause as the unusual outcome.

This is a direct example of:

```text
critical difference represented
BUT transfer veto/application fails
```

It therefore mirrors the general analogical dissociation seen elsewhere: **having the right information is not the same as using it to control transfer.**

## Projection-level label

```text
source pattern: 2025 repeated sanctions amendments
projection: another Q4 amendment
status: VETOED / strongly down-weighted
blocking condition: terminal deadline-driven package already completed
```

# Case 2 — Argentine peso: panic trajectory mistaken for a stable regime

## Target question

Would the official BCRA exchange rate depreciate at least 8% between 15 October and 31 December 2025?

## Recent pattern

The peso had depreciated roughly **6.5% in five days**, placing the 8% threshold apparently close.

## Model forecast

Claude Opus 4.6:

```text
85% Yes
```

largely extrapolating the recent trajectory.

## Target-side regime change

A midterm election was six days away and the incumbent was leading. The model's own rationale reportedly mentioned the election.

The election resolved favorably for the incumbent, after which the peso rallied roughly 10% the next day. Year-end depreciation was about 5%, below the threshold.

## Transfer diagnosis

The recent five-day trajectory was generated under a **panic / pre-election information regime**. It should not have been treated as an invariant local dynamic through the election boundary.

The relevant distinction is:

```text
trend continuation under same regime
```

vs

```text
forecast crosses a scheduled information/intervention boundary
→ transition dynamics may reset
```

## Projection-level label

```text
source pattern: recent rapid depreciation
projection: trend persists past election
status: CONDITIONAL / high uncertainty
boundary condition: imminent election can switch expectations and capital-flow regime
```

This is useful because the analogue is not a named historical event. It is a **recent trajectory precedent**, showing that the transfer-control problem applies across both rich event analogies and statistical historical patterns.

# Case 3 — NYC turnout: historical ceiling generated by an obsolete competitive structure

## Target question

Would the 2025 New York City mayoral general election reach **1.3 million total ballots**?

## Historical pattern

No NYC mayoral general election had reached that level since 2001.

A model could therefore construct a superficially strong 24-year reference-class ceiling.

## Model forecast

Claude Opus 4.6:

```text
25% Yes
```

anchored heavily on the historical turnout ceiling.

## Outcome

Actual turnout was approximately **2.2 million**.

## Structural break

The old ceiling was not merely a demographic regularity. It was partly generated by a long period of relatively uncompetitive general elections under a closed-primary environment that excluded a large block of voters from the decisive earlier contest.

The 2025 race differed structurally:

- an independent candidacy explicitly targeted the excluded electorate;
- the primary had already reached turnout comparable to recent general elections;
- competitive/mobilization structure was different from the period that produced the old ceiling.

The historical pattern therefore needed a conditional representation:

```text
turnout ceiling under low-competition / prior participation structure
```

rather than:

```text
NYC mayoral elections intrinsically stay below 1.3m
```

## Projection-level label

```text
source pattern: post-2001 turnout ceiling
projection: 2025 stays below 1.3m
status: VETOED / reference class requires redefinition
blocking condition: competitive/mobilization structure changed
```

# 4. Three distinct boundary types

These examples are valuable because they do not all instantiate the same error.

| Case | Pattern was real? | What changed? | Boundary type |
|---|---:|---|---|
| EU Iran sanctions | yes | process reached terminal externally driven completion | **driver exhaustion / terminal state** |
| Argentine peso | yes | imminent election changed information regime | **scheduled regime switch** |
| NYC turnout | yes | competitive and participation structure changed | **structural reference-class break** |

This suggests a historical transfer controller needs a vocabulary richer than `similar / dissimilar`.

Candidate boundary categories include:

```text
DRIVER_EXHAUSTED
REGIME_SWITCH
INSTITUTIONAL_CHANGE
TECHNOLOGICAL_CHANGE
ACTOR_SET_CHANGE
SCALE_CHANGE
INTERVENTION_BUFFER
SELECTION/REFERENCE_CLASS_CHANGE
CAUSAL_DIRECTION_CHANGE
TEMPORAL_PHASE_CHANGE
```

These categories can be learned/refined rather than hand-coded permanently.

---

# 5. Why these cases are stronger than ordinary hard negatives

In a standard hard-negative benchmark:

```text
source looks similar
but is actually wrong
```

These BTF-2 cases are harder:

```text
source pattern is genuinely predictive in earlier periods
AND the target initially resembles it
AND the model has evidence of the relevant difference
BUT applicability changed at the target cutoff
```

This tests **boundary-sensitive transfer**, not merely relational recognition.

---

# 6. A direct benchmark template

Each case can be transformed into four experimental arms.

## Arm A — pattern only

Give the model historical recurrence data without the new boundary evidence.

Expected: reasonable transfer/high probability may be defensible.

## Arm B — pattern + critical difference

Add the deadline/election/competition evidence.

Expected: the probability of the old projection should fall materially.

## Arm C — critical difference paraphrased / buried

Preserve the same evidence but make it less salient.

Measure whether the controller can retrieve the boundary condition rather than rely on prompt salience.

## Arm D — matched control where the boundary is absent

Construct another target where the historical generating mechanism remains active.

Expected: the controller should restore the precedent's usefulness rather than learn `historical recurrence is bad`.

This directly tests overgeneralized rejection.

---

# 7. Suggested metric: boundary response

For a historical projection `p`, define:

```text
ΔP_boundary = P(p | historical pattern only)
             - P(p | historical pattern + critical disanalogy)
```

Then compare against resolved outcome and expert/evidence-based expected direction.

A capable system should show:

- large appropriate probability change when the boundary matters;
- small change for irrelevant differences;
- restoration when the boundary is removed;
- good final Brier score.

This is closer to measuring the empirical `α_s^tr` problem than binary analogy classification.

---

# 8. Training implication

Resolved forecasting failures can automatically mine candidate boundary-memory examples.

Search for cases where:

```text
model forecast confidence is high
model rationale cites recurring historical pattern
outcome contradicts the pattern
pre-cutoff research contains evidence of a regime/boundary change
```

Then use an LLM/human pipeline to propose the discriminative condition and have experts validate the high-value subset.

BTF-2's full reasoning traces and frozen corpus make it unusually suitable for this mining strategy.

---

## Bottom line

These three BTF-2 failures provide direct real-world evidence for the narrowed historical-transfer frontier:

> **frontier models can identify genuine historical patterns and can even possess the evidence that those patterns have stopped applying, yet still fail to convert the difference into a calibrated veto/down-weighting of the projected outcome.**

This is precisely the gap between `difference awareness` and **operational transfer control**.
